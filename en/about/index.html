Working tools

The method adapts to the problem, not the other way around

SWOT and benchmarking

Understanding a business requires looking both inward and outward.

A SWOT analysis only becomes useful when it moves beyond a generic list of strengths and threats. At Synnergy Lab, it helps identify real capabilities, operating constraints, and advantages that can guide decisions. The aim is to separate what a business believes about itself from what it can genuinely sustain in the market.

Benchmarking completes that view by looking outward. It is not about copying competitors, but understanding how the market is positioned, what customers already expect, and where meaningful differentiation is possible. Together, both analyses create a stronger basis for deciding what to preserve, what to correct, and where to invest before execution begins.

Customer journey and funnel

Every action should serve a specific stage of the customer journey.

The customer journey shows how a person's needs change from discovering a brand to choosing to buy, return, or recommend it. Each stage brings different questions, friction points, and expectations. One message should not be expected to create awareness, earn trust, and drive conversion all at once.

The funnel turns that journey into an operating structure. It connects content, ads, pages, forms, follow-up, and sales so each touchpoint prepares the next. The goal is not to build a complicated funnel, but to reduce losses between stages and define exactly what every asset contributes to the commercial system.

Learning cycles

Execution produces data; analysis should produce better decisions.

A campaign does not end when it goes live. That moment begins a different phase: observing what happened, interpreting why, and separating a real signal from a temporary fluctuation. Isolated metrics rarely explain a business; they need context, comparison, and a clear relationship to the objectives set before execution.

Learning cycles turn that information into the next decision. A hypothesis is tested, measured, and refined through controlled changes. Each project then leaves reusable knowledge instead of only producing deliverables. Optimization becomes a permanent part of the system rather than an improvised reaction.
